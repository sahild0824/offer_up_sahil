## Bench construction with 5 bench slots

**QB2 in a 1QB league — consensus: skip it.**
- "With an elite quarterback as your anchor, you are very unlikely to need a backup quarterback and skipping one is actually recommended"; suggested base roster before Round 9: 1 QB, 3 RB, 4 WR. https://www.draftcall.io/preseason/draft-strategy/ (aggregated snippet; possibly https://fantasysixpack.net/fantasy-football-draft-strategies-from-pick-1/)
- "A surplus at quarterback or tight end is not useful, since both positions are replaceable enough off waivers that hoarding them wastes picks better spent elsewhere." https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/08/2026-fantasy-football-draft-strategy-guide-expert-advice/
- "a combination of two weekly starters at QB and another position beats a star QB paired with a bench piece." (aggregated snippet from the QB2 search; likely https://picks-s6.cbssports.com/fantasy/football/news/2026-fantasy-football-quarterback-rankings/)
- FantasyPros' QB2 piece frames stashing a QB2 as for "deeper leagues and superflex formats or... if you carry multiple quarterbacks." https://www.fantasypros.com/2026/09/5-qb2s-with-top-5-potential-2026-fantasy-football/
- Dissent (forum): a Footballguys Forums poster "usually want[s] a backup QB because in most real fantasy leagues they see other teams doing the same which means the QB pool really thins out" — but that was with a 10-man bench. https://forums.footballguys.com/threads/how-do-you-like-your-bench-players.817330/

**Handcuff your own RB1 — split.**
- Pro: "Handcuffing your top running back is 'the single most underrated move in fantasy football'... Starting running backs miss time at a rate of roughly 30% over a 17-game season." https://www.draftsharks.com/article/fantasy-football-draft-strategy-guide
- Con: "Some analysts don't love handcuffing in the modern era, especially handcuffing your own running backs. There are very few instances where it's worth dedicating two roster spots to 'insurance.' Alternatively, you can draft backups to RBs you don't have on your roster, which actually gives your rosters more potential upside." https://www.fantasyalarm.com/articles/nfl/fantasy-football-advice/2026-fantasy-football-best-handcuffs-and-contigent-upside-plays/194447
- Con: "not handcuffing your running backs — at least not yet." https://sports.yahoo.com/fantasy/article/2026-fantasy-football-rb-preview-draft-strategy-rankings-fades-and-sleepers-163344738.html ; "handcuffing isn't bad strategy, but automatically handcuffing is bad strategy." https://ftnfantasy.com/nfl/fantasy-football-handcuff-strategy-for-2026

**RB vs WR on the bench — RB-heavy.**
- "You should be saving late-round picks for as many injury-away running backs as you can... some experts want almost their entire bench full of these guys. It's not uncommon to spend the last five picks exclusively on these players." (aggregated snippet; likely https://www.footballguys.com/article/2026-comprehensive-guide-to-dominate-your-league-fantasy-football-guide or https://www.cbssports.com/fantasy/football/news/jamey-eisenbergs-2026-fantasy-draft-plan/)
- Best-ball counterpoint (rounds 15-18): "fill out wide receivers, avoid speculative shots with high zero risk (particularly at RB and QB), and target tight ends with locked-in roles." https://www.rotowire.com/football/article/underdog-best-ball-fantasy-football-late-round-targets-108805 (best ball only; not directly applicable to a 5-bench redraft)

**Applied to our 5 bench picks (84/97/104/117 + one of the D/ST-K slots is not bench):** the expert consensus supports 3-4 RBs (one being a standalone-value handcuff like Corum, the rest contingent backs — Washington, Black, Mason, Allgeier), 1-2 WR darts (Lane/Douglas/Stribling), and no QB2/TE2 unless Kittle is the TE1 gamble (then Likely/Kincaid as the pairing).
